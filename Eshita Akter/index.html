01 — Who Is Eshita

From reconciling ledgers to reading data for a living.

My career started in the exacting world of financial compliance — checking transactions, verifying visa documents, and making sure regulatory reports were airtight. It's work with no room for a wrong number, and it taught me to read data the way an auditor does: carefully, skeptically, and always asking what it actually proves.

Over time I noticed the part of the job I looked forward to most wasn't the checklist — it was the pattern underneath it. Why did errors cluster in certain accounts? Which process step quietly caused the most rework? That curiosity is what pulled me toward SQL, Python and Power BI, and eventually toward an MBA in Finance to formalize the business lens I'd been building on the job.

Today I sit at the intersection of finance, operations and analytics — someone who can validate a dataset, question its assumptions, and turn it into a dashboard a decision-maker will actually use. I'm not chasing analytics for its own sake; I'm chasing better decisions.

05 — Projects

Case studies, not just charts.

Python · Power BI · DAX

U.S. Public Pension Plans Analysis

Business question: Are U.S. public pension systems on track to meet their long-term obligations, and how did funding recover after 2008?

Built an interactive financial dashboard analyzing 230+ U.S. public pension systems (2001–2023), calculating funded ratios and long-term asset-vs-liability trends. Cleaned and transformed large multi-year datasets in Python, then developed KPI and state-level visualizations to surface funding disparities and post-2008 recovery patterns.

Approach: Multi-year dataset cleaning in Python → funded-ratio calculations → state-level KPI dashboard in Power BI with DAX measures.

Insight: Funding disparities across states became visible only after normalizing for asset-vs-liability trends over time.

Advanced Ride-Sharing Analysis — Uber & Lyft, Boston

Business question: When and where does ride-sharing demand peak, and how should pricing respond?

Analyzed ride-sharing trip data to identify demand trends, peak hours and high-demand pricing zones. Applied time-series and geospatial analysis to forecast hourly ride demand, then built interactive dashboards and heatmaps for comparative fare and surge-pricing analysis.

Approach: Time-series decomposition of trip volume + geospatial clustering of pickup zones → surge-pricing heatmap.

Insight: Peak-hour and zone-level patterns pointed to specific windows where surge pricing had the most business impact.

06 — How I Think

From question to insight.

01

Understand the Business Problem

Before opening a dataset, I get clear on what decision this analysis is actually meant to support.

02

Ask the Right Questions

Compliance work trained me to interrogate a number before trusting it — where did it come from, and what could make it wrong?

03

Explore the Data

Clean, validate and profile the data first. Most real insight hides behind messy, inconsistent records.

04

Find Patterns & Gaps

Look for what's consistent, what's an outlier, and what the data quietly isn't telling you.

05

Communicate Insights

Turn analysis into a dashboard or narrative a non-technical stakeholder can act on immediately.

06

Support Better Decisions

The goal was never the chart — it was the decision the chart made easier.
